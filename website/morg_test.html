Neutron Stars

Neutron stars are a dense compact objects mostly comprised of neutrons due to electrons occupying all possible energy levels in something known as "fermi energy". Masses for these objects range from 1.4 M☉ to approximently 2.2 M☉ (where M☉ represents solar mass, which is the mass of our solar system's sun). These compact objects are formed from Core Collaspe SuperNova (CCSN), a process which I will detail within its own section, but for simplicity, these SuperNova(SN) occur when the internal pressure of a high mass star can no longer support against gravitional collapse, leaving behind a compact core which can either be a neutron star or a black hole.
